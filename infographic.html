Spec §4  — the boundary

Two databases, one server, no joins across

The single most important thing to see first is what retail is not connected to. Marmyadose's dao already contains a table called pos_master_produk — a completely different one, serving Nick Cell and Layescent. Both databases live on this same MySQL server, so MySQL would happily let you join them. The design forbids it, because that join is what would trap the schema when the backend moves to Bun.js.

Spec §5  — how the tables connect

One hub, three families hanging off it

Every table resolves to the same shape: sy_perusahaan is the tenant root, pos_master_produk is the hub that almost everything points at, and the rest divides into lookups above it and transactional data below it. Arrows run from child to parent — the direction a foreign key actually points.

Why no foreign key on rekap_id

pos_stok_mutasi carries rekap_tipe and rekap_id pointing at whichever document caused the movement — a receipt, a transfer, a conversion. Those document tables don't exist yet, and one column can't hold a foreign key to five different tables. It is an intentional soft link, indexed as (rekap_tipe, rekap_id).

Spec §5.5  — pricing

A price is found, not stored on the product

The old schema had one HARGA column on the product, which could not express selling the same bottle at one price in a shop, another through GoFood, and a third when the factory transfers it to an outlet. Price now lives in its own table, keyed by product, level and region — and the region is allowed to be NULL, meaning "applies everywhere".

The one asymmetry worth remembering

NULL means two opposite things in two places, on purpose. On pos_master_produk.barcode, NULL means "this product has no barcode" — so many rows may be NULL and MySQL's default behaviour is exactly right. On pos_harga_produk.region_id, NULL carries meaning — "applies to every region" — so it must be collapsed to a sentinel before being indexed. Same database, opposite treatment, both deliberate.

Spec §5.6  — stock

The ledger is the truth; the balance is a cache of it

Stock is never edited in place. Every movement appends a row to pos_stok_mutasi carrying a signed jumlah and the resulting stok_akhir, and the same transaction bumps the cached balance in pos_stok_outlet. Because the balance after every single movement is recorded, you can replay the ledger and prove the cache has not drifted — something nothing in the legacy schema could do.

Direction lives in the sign

Not in tipe. A conversion is two rows sharing one rekap_id — −1 on the Box product and +12 on the Pcs product. A transfer is two rows at two outlets. The type says why; jumlah says which way.

Drift is detectable

Replaying SUM(jumlah) for a product must land on the cached stok. If it doesn't, something wrote outside the transaction. The legacy tables offered nothing to check against anything.

Adjustments are gated

penyesuaian is the only type that sets an absolute quantity. The server computes jumlah from the current balance and rejects the write unless sy_karyawan.peran is auditor or admin. Never available to staff.

What the sample data was checked against

Seven assertions, all passing on the full 46 869 movements: cached balance equals SUM(jumlah) for every one of the 13 126 balance rows; stok_akhir equals the running total when the ledger is replayed in chronological order, not merely insert order; no sale goes negative without an alasan_minus; no adjustment is written by anyone below auditor; no movement mixes one company's outlet with another's product or staff; every paired document has exactly two rows; and no rekap_id is reused between the two companies.

Two things about this data that are not real

rekap_id values point at nothing — the document tables they will reference belong to the next module and do not exist yet. And 266 of the 581 products carry no barcode, which is deliberate: it exercises the NULL-barcode case that a stricter unique index would have broken.

Reading the data  — eight views

Names instead of ids

Reading the raw tables means holding six lookups in your head at once — satuan_id 3 tells you nothing. Eight views resolve every foreign key to the name it points at, so a row can be read on its own. They are for browsing and learning; application queries should still hit the tables directly and select only what they need.

ViewWhat it gives you
v_produkThe master item with every id spelled out — unit, category, brand, default supplier, who created it. Start here.
v_produk_hargaOne row per product, its entire price list as one readable string: Retail: 2.600 | Retail [BAL]: 3.200 | GoFood: 3.500
v_harga_produkOne row per price — level, whether it is company-wide or regional, and the formatted amount. Long form, easy to filter.
v_konversiRecipes as a sentence: 1 Box 11002B Air Mineral 330ml → 24 Botol 11002 Air Mineral 330ml [Berkah Tani]
v_stok_outletBalances with outlet, region and product named, plus a kondisi flag — minus, kosong, menipis, aman.
v_stok_mutasiThe ledger in plain language — who moved what, which way, what was left, and which document it came from.
v_pending_terimaEverything sold before its receipt was filed, with the reason, the note and the person attached.
v_ringkasan_produkOne row per product: how many prices, how many outlets carry it, total stock, how many outlets are negative, movement count.

sequence is not the price level

The level is the row — pos_level_harga.nama holds Retail, GoFood, Transfer Pabrik, and that is what a price is keyed by. sequence only controls the order the levels appear in a list. It is not a precedence rank and nothing resolves prices with it. In v_harga_produk it is renamed urutan_tampil to say so out loud.

Twenty columns across the schema now carry a COMMENT explaining themselves, readable straight from the database — and MySQL copies them through into the views:

-- what does this column actually mean?
SELECT table_name, column_name, column_comment
FROM information_schema.columns
WHERE table_schema='retail' AND column_comment <> '';
Subscription  — the SaaS layer

Paid for, or locked out

The POS is sold yearly, billed on outlets and staff. Four tables sit above the schema and answer two questions at all times: has this company paid, and are they inside what they paid for.

TableRoleScope
sy_pricingFlat price per unit per month. The landing page and the renewal calculation read the same row, so they cannot disagree.global
sy_subscriptionCurrent term and quota. One row per company. The four triggers read this.per company
sy_payment_rekapOne row per renewal — the document header.per company
sy_payment_detailThe lines. harga_per_bulan is snapshotted, so raising prices never rewrites history.per company

Hard lock has a cost, and it was chosen with eyes open

There is no grace state: a transfer clearing one day late shuts the shop, and the lock covers the company's own admin — so a lapsed customer cannot even see what they owe from inside the app. Renewal is entirely out of band. The escape hatch is not a state but an edit: a human moves berlaku_sampai and writes why in catatan.

Verified by database/06_subscription_check.php — 16 assertions, half of which must be refused: hiring past quota, opening an outlet past quota, reactivating at quota, a company with no subscription creating its first outlet, a mis-computed payment line, a reversed period, a duplicate price and a negative quota. All pass, and every one rolls back.

Next  — what is not in the database yet

Deliberately absent

Spec §5.7 defines the document tables as a contract only, and hands their implementation to the next module. They were not created — which is why rekap_tipe and rekap_id carry no foreign key today.

Document tables — next module

pos_masuk_rekap / pos_masuk_detail, and the same pair for keluar, transfer, konversi and retail. Stock moves only when a document is posted; a void reverses by writing opposing movements, never by deleting ledger rows.

Still open

Whether pos_region should allow private per-company regions; whether stock reservation is needed in v1; and how sy_karyawan relates to Marmyadose's users and pos_users for login — the one genuine seam between the two databases.

Deferred, all additive

Multiple barcodes per product, per-outlet product availability, scheduled price changes, purchase orders and supplier banking, a fuller permission model, and range-partitioning the ledger by year. None of them require reworking what exists.
